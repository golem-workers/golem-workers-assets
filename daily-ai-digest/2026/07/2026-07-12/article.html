This is the GolemWorkers Daily AI Digest, a concise briefing on practical agents and the products putting them to work. Today: ChatGPT tackles connected projects, Mistral moves robots with one camera, Meta advances agentic coding, and Lyzr puts an agent on investor diligence.

ChatGPT Work takes on connected projects that run for hours

OpenAI says its new ChatGPT Work agent can gather context from connected email, calendars, team messages, files, and business apps, then produce finished documents, spreadsheets, presentations, sites, and recurring updates. It is rolling out on web and mobile to Pro, Enterprise, and Edu users first, with Plus and Business access following over the next few days.

Mistral gives robots one-camera, plain-language navigation

Robostral Navigate lets a robot follow a multi-step route through offices, homes, commercial buildings, or outdoor spaces using one ordinary RGB camera instead of LiDAR or depth sensors. Mistral says the 8-billion-parameter model works across wheeled, legged, and flying robots and can adapt around people and obstacles it did not encounter in training.

Meta launches Muse Spark 1.1 for agentic coding and computer use

Meta publicly released Muse Spark 1.1, a multimodal model designed to plan across external tools, operate software, fix bugs, and help with large code migrations. The model is available through Meta's API for teams building coding and workflow agents, with usage priced by input and output tokens.

Lyzr puts an AI agent on the front line of a $100 million fundraise

Enterprise-agent startup Lyzr says its SivaClaw system fielded questions from more than 130 prospective investors, drafted investment memos, and tracked which slides drew attention during its Series B process. The company reported $400 million in interest; the round was still being described through company and media accounts rather than a completed financing announcement.

Meta removes Instagram's public-photo AI remix feature after backlash

Meta withdrew a Muse Image feature that let people reference public Instagram accounts when generating images. Eligible accounts were not notified when their photos were used, and the setting had been enabled by default; Meta said the feature missed the mark and is no longer available.

Agent idea of the day

A move-day concierge for local government services

What this agent does

Coordinate every approved municipal task triggered by a resident address change without making eligibility or identity decisions.

Best for: City and county service teams that handle address changes across several departments.

Give it

  • An approved intake form and identity-verification result
  • Current service directory, rules, forms, and department contacts
  • A permissioned case-management workspace and staff approval queue

Tell it to

  1. Watch the approved intake queue for a verified address-change case.
  2. Extract the old and new address, move date, household details, and requested services.
  3. Map the case to the relevant departments, forms, deadlines, and supporting documents.
  4. Draft each update, flag missing information, and place every action in the staff approval queue.
  5. Record approvals, submit only authorized actions, and send the resident one consolidated status update.

Run it: Run when a verified address-change case enters the queue, then check daily until every approved task is closed.

You get

One case brief with required actions, missing documents, deadlines, approval status, and a resident-ready update.

Keep a human in control

  • Use only approved government systems and current policy documents.
  • Never decide eligibility, verify identity, move money, or submit a form without the designated staff approval.
  • Minimize personal data, keep an audit log, and escalate conflicting records or uncertain rules.

Feasibility: Japan's internal affairs ministry is studying agents for understaffed local governments and specifically cites the tasks that follow a resident address change, with employee approval and monitoring. The workflow relies on common document extraction, rules lookup, task routing, drafting, and approval-queue capabilities. Source: The Japan Times →
