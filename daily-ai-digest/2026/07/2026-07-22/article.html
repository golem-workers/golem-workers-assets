Agent idea of the day

Build a morning opportunity scout for contracts and grants

What this agent does

Monitor the public sources that matter to your business, verify new opportunities and deliver a short evidence-backed list before deadlines become urgent—without contacting buyers or submitting anything.

Best for: Small contractors, agencies, consultants and nonprofits that cannot spend hours checking scattered bid, grant and procurement portals.

Give it

  • A fixed list of official portals, buyer pages and grant sources to monitor
  • Your service area, capabilities, minimum and maximum project size, required certifications and no-go sectors
  • A calendar of blackout dates plus the person who decides whether an opportunity moves forward

Tell it to

  1. Check only the approved source list and record every new or changed notice with its canonical URL.
  2. Extract the buyer, scope, location, value, eligibility rules, required documents, question deadline and submission deadline.
  3. Score each opportunity against the provided geography, capabilities, capacity, certifications and deal-size limits, citing the evidence behind every score.
  4. Reopen the official notice before reporting it, flag amendments or missing documents and discard expired, duplicated or aggregator-only listings.
  5. Prepare a ranked brief with the five strongest fits, the first three actions for each and a clear approval request; never contact a buyer or submit a response.

Run it: Run at 7:00 a.m. every weekday and immediately when an approved portal posts an amendment to a tracked opportunity.

You get

A one-page opportunity brief with verified links, fit scores, deadlines, missing requirements, recommended next steps and an explicit pursue-or-pass queue for a person.

Keep a human in control

  • Treat the official notice and its attachments as the source of truth; never rely on an aggregator for dates or eligibility.
  • Never contact buyers, register an account, make promises, set pricing or submit a bid or application without explicit approval.
  • Do not invent certifications, past performance, team capacity or financial information; mark unknowns visibly.
  • Escalate legal terms, bonding, insurance, conflicts, unusual data requests and any deadline ambiguity to a person.

Feasibility: Cascade’s launched service monitors state, city, district, county, federal and private construction sources, connects prior tender data and recommends projects a firm is positioned to win. That live workflow supports a narrower, safer agent that monitors an approved source list, verifies canonical notices, scores fit and prepares a human decision brief without outreach or submission authority.
