Grok Bot turns persistent agents into always-on digital coworkers

The early beta lets people create bots with their own cloud computers, give them access to selected apps and websites, schedule recurring work and coordinate handoffs with other bots. They keep working when the user's laptop is closed and return for approval or with a finished result; access begins with SuperGrok Heavy, Cursor Ultra and Premium Teams subscribers.

Ahrefs launches Letaido for recurring marketing research and monitoring

Letaido gives marketers and agencies an agent workspace for multistep jobs such as competitor research, content audits, campaign analysis and recurring monitoring. Teams can schedule workflows, review live progress and use Ahrefs data alongside connections to tools including Notion, Slack, HubSpot, Google Ads and WordPress.

Assort Health's referral agent follows patients through to a booked appointment

Now generally available, Referrals processes incoming documents, confirms eligibility, matches the right provider and reaches patients by voice, text or email until an appointment is booked in the health record. It keeps the referring office informed and routes cases that need clinical judgment back to staff.

Claude Code keeps remote and cross-session work from dropping between turns

Claude Code 2.1.228 improves work that spans sessions: background tasks can finish while a remote runner waits for the next instruction, and cross-session messages no longer disappear after the receiving session is compacted. Agent idea of the day

Build a sales follow-up agent that prepares the handoff, then waits

What this agent does

Turn each completed sales call into an evidence-backed CRM update and personalized follow-up packet while keeping every write and message behind a human approval step.

Best for: Founders and small sales teams that lose context between calls, account research, CRM updates and promised follow-ups.

Give it

  • The meeting transcript and attendee list
  • Read-only access to the account record and approved research sources
  • Your ideal-customer rubric, CRM field definitions and writing examples
  • A list of approved assets the team can promise or attach

Tell it to

  1. Extract the commitments, objections, stakeholders and next dates from the transcript and cite the exact supporting passages.
  2. Verify account and contact facts in the approved systems, marking anything uncertain instead of guessing.
  3. Draft the CRM update as a field-by-field before-and-after review without writing it to the live record.
  4. Draft a concise follow-up email and optional LinkedIn note in the team's voice, including only assets that were actually promised.
  5. Assemble one approval packet and wait for explicit confirmation before writing to the CRM or sending any message.

Run it: Run when an approved meeting transcript arrives and return the packet within 15 minutes; stay silent when a transcript is incomplete or consent is unclear.

You get

One review packet containing transcript evidence, verified account facts, a CRM field diff, ready-to-edit follow-up drafts, promised assets and a clear list of actions awaiting approval.

Keep a human in control

  • Use only approved systems and public sources, and record where every external fact came from.
  • Never invent a commitment, contact detail, buying signal or next step.
  • Do not write to the CRM, send email, post a message or create a task without explicit human approval for that action.
  • Keep sensitive transcript content out of drafts unless it is necessary and approved for the recipient.
  • Log approved changes and deliveries so a person can reconstruct what happened.

Feasibility: VentureBeat's August 11 report describes internal Grok Bot sales workflows that add call-transcript notes to CRM records, research accounts and prepare email or LinkedIn outreach for approval.
