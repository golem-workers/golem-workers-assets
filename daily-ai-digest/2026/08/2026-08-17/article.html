This is the GolemWorkers daily AI digest on agents that carry work across tools while staying accountable to people. Today, managed OpenClaw and Hermes reach Cloudways, enterprises gain new agent control layers, and an AI-generated code fix becomes a security warning.

Cloudways launches managed OpenClaw and Hermes

Cloudways has made OpenClaw and Hermes generally available as managed AI agents for developers and businesses. Customers can deploy either from the Cloudways control panel while the service handles provisioning, isolated Docker environments, SSL and maintenance; an MCP connection can also let the agents act on existing Cloudways servers and applications.

xpander.ai has made its agent control plane generally available for enterprises running agents across different models and frameworks. Wiz shows how an AI code fix opened a path to Jira

Wiz disclosed that an AI-coauthored Copilot Autofix patch removed a safe sanitization pattern from a Snowflake CI workflow and created a command-injection route that its Red Agent found. Snowflake patched the issue and rotated credentials the same day, then said its review found no unauthorized access—making the episode a concrete warning to review agent-generated fixes like any other privileged change.

A phone call can reach your laptop’s coding agents

Call My Laptop is an early-access Mac app that gives the computer a phone number, then lets a user start or resume Codex, Claude Code or OpenCode sessions by voice while away from the desk. The laptop calls back when work finishes or the agent needs input; it must remain awake and online, and existing sessions stay on the Mac.

Agent idea of the day

Build a contract renewal radar before deadlines become emergencies

What this agent does

Watch agreements for renewal and notice dates, then prepare a concise renew, renegotiate or cancel brief before the decision window closes.

Best for: Founders, operators, finance teams and legal teams overseeing recurring software, vendor, lease or service agreements.

Give it

  • Contracts, amendments and vendor correspondence
  • Current usage, spend and service-issue records
  • The named owner for each agreement
  • One approved calendar and notification channel

Tell it to

  1. Index every agreement and amendment, extracting the renewal date, notice window, pricing, owner and termination terms.
  2. Recheck the contract folder and vendor correspondence weekly, and flag conflicting dates or missing documents.
  3. Start a review 90 days before the earliest notice deadline, comparing usage, spend, alternatives and unresolved service issues.
  4. Draft a one-page renew, renegotiate or cancel brief with source links, uncertainty and the last safe decision date.
  5. Ask the named owner to approve any reminder, vendor outreach or calendar change, then log the decision and next review date.

Run it: Run weekly, immediately when a new contract or amendment arrives, and 90 days before the earliest notice deadline.

You get

A one-page decision brief, a verified deadline calendar and one approval request for the named owner.

Keep a human in control

  • Never accept, renew, cancel or contact a vendor without explicit human approval.
  • Do not provide legal advice; route ambiguous or high-risk language to qualified counsel.
  • Link every extracted term to its source document and mark conflicts or missing evidence.
  • Use least-privilege access and keep an audit log of reminders, approvals and changes.

Feasibility: Sansan’s new contract agent can search across agreements and review drafts against past contracts and internal standards, demonstrating that corpus-wide extraction and comparison are practical.
