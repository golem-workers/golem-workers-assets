Alibaba takes its QwenWork desktop agent global

QwenWork International is now in public beta for individuals and enterprises, combining web navigation, computer control, deep research, office documents, scheduling, authorized local-file work and even website building and deployment in one agent. It currently supports English and Simplified Chinese, while the desktop client adds opt-in memory across sessions and lets successful workflows be saved as reusable skills.

Meta tests a personal agent that can run everyday errands

Meta is testing Project Hatch with employees, giving the agent its own computer so it can research, fill out forms, book restaurants, shop and find a dog sitter through connected services such as email, calendars and OpenTable. The test is not public, and Meta has not confirmed a launch date or price, so consumers cannot use it yet.

OpenAI tests a Codex mode that keeps finding work to do

Public Codex repository changes describe a Persistent mode that can continue until put to sleep, create follow-up tasks and carry work across sessions using prior context. OpenAI confirmed the test to WIRED but says there is no immediate launch plan; the draft instructions also say persistence does not expand permissions and external changes still require approval.

Liveops adds a real-time agent beside customer-service teams

LiveNexus Agent Assist is a browser overlay that follows live customer interactions, surfaces relevant knowledge, recommends the next action, enforces compliance steps and automates data entry and call wrap-up without replacing a company’s existing CRM or contact-center platform. It is available now to Liveops clients and prospective partners, with role controls and auditable interaction records for regulated environments.

Cashfree’s Relay works the payment problems small teams postpone

Relay is now available to all Cashfree merchants and can retry failed payments, follow up on abandoned carts, confirm cash-on-delivery orders, manage failed subscriptions and file disputes before deadlines. Merchants describe the outcome by voice or prompt, retain visibility into every action and must approve before money moves or a customer is contacted; the service is free at launch.

Agent idea of the day

Build a payment-exception desk that asks before acting

What this agent does

Turn daily payment failures, abandoned checkouts and looming dispute deadlines into one prioritized approval queue before revenue leaks become customer problems.

Best for: Founders, finance leads and operations managers at small online businesses that still review payment exceptions by hand.

Give it

  • Read-only exports from your payment provider, store and subscription system
  • Your retry, refund, dispute and customer-contact policies
  • Approved message templates and customer consent rules
  • One named reviewer who alone can authorize money movement or outbound contact

Tell it to

  1. Collect new failed payments, abandoned checkouts, subscription errors, cash-on-delivery exceptions and dispute deadlines.
  2. Group related records, remove duplicates and rank each exception by value, urgency and available evidence.
  3. Check every proposed action against the business policy and flag missing consent, identity mismatches or uncertain transaction context.
  4. Draft the smallest recommended action and any customer message without executing either one.
  5. Present one approval packet with source links, deadlines and reversible options, then apply only the items the named reviewer accepts.

Run it: Run every weekday morning and immediately when a dispute deadline is less than 24 hours away.

You get

A prioritized exception board with evidence, recommended next action, draft customer copy, deadline and a separate approval control for every money-moving or outbound step.

Keep a human in control

  • Never move money, retry a charge, issue a refund, file a dispute or contact a customer without explicit approval from the named reviewer.
  • Use tokenized transaction references only; never store full card data, passwords or payment-provider secrets in the work queue.
  • Do not infer consent, customer identity or fraud; stop and escalate whenever records conflict.
  • Keep an append-only record of sources, recommendations, approvals and executed results so every action can be audited.

Feasibility: Cashfree says Relay currently handles failed payments, abandoned carts, cash-on-delivery confirmations, subscription failures and dispute deadlines while requiring merchant approval before money moves or a customer is contacted.
